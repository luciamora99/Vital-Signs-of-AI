    Patient history, 1950 to 2022

    1950s–60s · Birth

    • 1950Turing proposes the Turing Test.
    • 1956Dartmouth Conference names "artificial intelligence."
    • 1958Rosenblatt's Perceptron, an early neural network.
    • 1959Arthur Samuel coins "machine learning."
    • 1964–66ELIZA imitates a therapist.MED
    • 1969Perceptrons shows the limits of simple neural networks.

    1970s–80s · Winters

    • 1972MYCIN diagnoses blood infections, but never reaches hospitals.MED
    • 1974–1980First AI winter: weak computers, failed promises, funding cut.
    • 1980Expert systems like XCON go commercial.
    • 1986Backpropagation makes deep networks trainable.
    • 1987–1993Second AI winter: brittle expert systems, hardware market collapses.

    Our reflection

    What stood out to me is how fast AI moved in just four years. It went from ChatGPT’s launch to models that pass the bar exam, win gold at the Math Olympiad, and even win Nobel Prizes. But the ethical events tell a different story. Lawsuits, safety warnings, and cases like UnitedHealth show what happens when AI decisions replace human judgment, and the laws are still catching up.

    Coming from UX, accessibility, and data backgrounds, I think the real question isn't how smart the AI is, but who it's designed for and who is accountable when it gets it wrong.
